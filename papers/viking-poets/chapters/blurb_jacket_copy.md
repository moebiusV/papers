# Jacket copy / blurb

A tease, not a summary: one surprising equivalence, a promise, and a close that
lets the right reader feel like an insider.

---

## Primary (~140 words)

A skald composing in tenth-century Iceland and an engineer at Bell Labs in 1950
were working on the same problem, and they reached the same answer: structure
is redundancy, and redundancy is how a signal survives a channel that wants to
corrupt it. One of them worked in kennings, alliteration, and meter. The other
worked in parity bits.

Take that equivalence seriously and it runs a long way: through the scribes who
counted every letter of the Hebrew Bible, the reciters who held the Vedas
intact for three thousand years, and on to the machine on your desk that writes
with perfect fluency and no idea when it is lying.

If you keep *Gödel, Escher, Bach* and *Hackers & Painters* within reach, and
have always half-suspected that the old crafts and the new mathematics were
describing the same thing, you were right. This is the book that shows it.

---

## Shorter alternate (~85 words)

A tenth-century Viking skald and a 1950s engineer at Bell Labs were solving the
same problem, and they reached the same answer. One used kennings and meter;
the other used parity bits. This book follows that equivalence, from the poets
who kept knowledge alive across a thousand years of faulty memory to the
machine on your desk that writes with perfect fluency and no idea when it is
lying. If you already suspected the old crafts and the new mathematics were the
same craft, you were right.
