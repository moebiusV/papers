# Jacket copy / blurb — aimed at the three-layer audience

**~150 words**

Everyone in AI is asking how to make the model bigger. This book asks the
question underneath that one: how do you build an intelligence that cannot
tell when it is wrong?

For three thousand years the answer has been the same. The Norse skalds, the
Masoretes who counted every letter of the Hebrew Bible, the Brahmins who
carried the Vedas bit-perfect across millennia, the hadith scholars who built
a formal science of provenance — each of them, independently, wrapped the
fallible brain in the same six-layer architecture of error correction. Modern
AI has built one of those layers and is trying to scale it into the other
five.

Part information theory, part comparative philology, *What the Viking Poets
Knew* traces a single deep idea across domains that were never meant to touch —
the way *Gödel, Escher, Bach* once did. For readers who suspect the ancients
solved something we have forgotten, and who want it shown, not asserted.
